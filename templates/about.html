2. Project Objectives & Screening Boundary

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading contributor to global mortality. Electrocardiography (ECG) is the foundational non-invasive clinical modality used by cardiologists to evaluate cardiac electrical conduction, ischemic injury, and rhythm abnormalities.

Primary engineering objectives of this academic capstone:

  • Accept single-lead (Lead MLII) continuous ECG recordings from ambulatory monitoring telemetry.
  • Eliminate respiration baseline wander (< 0.5 Hz) and high-frequency electromyographic noise (> 45 Hz) via zero-phase digital IIR filtering.
  • Accurately localize ventricular QRS centroids and calculate heart rate (BPM) across variable ambulatory rhythm strips.
  • Classify segmented cardiac beats into 4 standard AAMI classes using a lightweight 1D CNN with inter-patient generalization.
  • Present findings through an educational research dashboard strictly designed as a preliminary triage screening prototype.

3. PhysioNet MIT-BIH Dataset & Inter-Patient Partition

Zero Data Leakage Protocol

The project benchmarks on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database from PhysioNet, consisting of 48 two-channel ambulatory ECG recordings from 47 distinct human subjects, digitized at 360 Hz with 11-bit resolution over a 10 mV dynamic range.

Data Leakage Prevention (De Chazal et al. Standard)

Randomly splitting individual heartbeats from the same patient across training and testing sets produces severe data leakage. The neural network simply memorizes patient-specific electrode impedance and claims false >99% accuracy that collapses on new individuals.

To evaluate genuine clinical generalization, this system implements the inter-patient split:
• DS1 (Training & Validation Cohort): Records 101, 106, 119, 201, 208, 209 (13,795 heartbeats)
• DS2 (Completely Unseen Test Patients): Records 100, 103, 105, 111, 200, 214 (13,910 heartbeats)

4. 1D CNN Architecture & Parameter Efficiency

44,452 Parameters (~173 KB)

Built in TensorFlow and Keras, the 1D CNN ingests 360-sample temporal vectors representing 1.0 second of Lead MLII centered on the QRS centroid:

Layer Name Output Tensor Shape Kernel / Stride Biomedical Signal Function
Input Layer (None, 360, 1) - Standardized 1.0s cardiac cycle centered at sample 180.
Conv1D Block 1 (None, 180, 32) k=7, MaxPool=2 Extracts fast QRS upstroke slopes and sharp temporal deflections.
Conv1D Block 2 (None, 90, 64) k=5, MaxPool=2 Assembles low-level edges into sub-wave detectors (P-wave, QRS duration, T-wave).
Conv1D Block 3 (None, 45, 128) k=3, MaxPool=2 Captures multi-wave temporal patterns (PR-interval duration, ST elevation).
Global Avg Pool (None, 128) Global Pool Averages temporal dimensions to eliminate dense layer parameter explosion.
Dense + Dropout (None, 64) Dropout=0.35 Non-linear projection with regularization to prevent feature co-adaptation.
Output Layer (None, 4) Softmax Calculates calibrated probability distribution across the 4 AAMI classes.

6. Technical Limitations & Future Scope

Current System Limitations

  • Single-Lead Input: Model operates on Modified Limb Lead II (MLII). In hospital diagnostics, 12 simultaneous leads are required for full spatial axis analysis.
  • SVEB Class Imbalance: SVEB beats in ambulatory databases are sparse and preserve normal QRS width, requiring larger multi-lead cohorts.
  • Electrode Detachment Artifacts: Severe patient shivering or loose electrodes can generate voltage spikes resembling ectopic beats.

Future Research Roadmap

  • Extension to 12-lead multi-channel input (PTB-XL database).
  • Integration of temporal self-attention (1D CNN + Conformer/Transformer).
  • Quantization to TensorFlow Lite Micro for deployment on ultra-low-power wearable microcontrollers.
